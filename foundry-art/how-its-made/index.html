How we make our tiles

Bronze, worked by hand — the way it has been for thousands of years. Every Foundry Art tile is cast from a single original carving and finished one at a time.

Respect the past

An alloy five thousand years old.

Bronze is copper and tin — first combined some five thousand years ago [verify], and worked much the same way ever since. Cast solid, it holds detail no coating can, and it only grows richer with handling and age. It is the same alloy used to cast fine sculpture, and we treat it that way.

Before a line is carved

A design you’ll live with for years.

We sit with a new pattern for weeks, sometimes months, until it’s one we’d want to see every day for decades. Each drawing sets a traditional motif against a modern eye, so the finished tile reads as neither dated nor of-the-moment — just right, for a long time.

From wax to finished bronze

The work of many hands.

A pattern is carved a single time, in wax. Every tile after it is cast from that original — a true copy of the first carving, not a copy of a copy. From there, each piece passes through many hands before it ships.

Look toward the future

It only gets better from here.

Because it’s solid and unsealed, bronze keeps changing after it leaves us — darkening, and catching light with a depth flat finishes can’t. How each alloy ages, and how to care for it, is covered in Care & Technical Details.
