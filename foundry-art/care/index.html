A living finish

Bronze becomes more beautiful with age.

Solid bronze isn’t sealed behind a coating, so it keeps changing after it leaves us. Over the years it both darkens and brightens — mellowing like a treasured antique, and catching light with a depth flat finishes can’t. The patina isn’t a flaw to prevent; it’s the material doing what bronze has done for five thousand years. [verify]

How far it goes is up to you: let it age, slow it down, or hold it close to the day it arrived. Here’s how each alloy behaves — and how to steer it.

By alloy

How each alloy ages.

Traditional Bronze

An alloy of copper and tin — the same used to cast fine sculpture. It arrives with the warm luster of newly cast and buffed bronze, then develops a rich, mellow patina over the years, growing darker and deeper than the day it arrived. A light buffing brings the brightness back whenever you want it.

Photo to source: new → ~20-year patina → after light buffing

White Bronze

Less copper, more zinc and nickel — a cooler color close to nickel silver. It starts with a cool luster and ages more subtly, staying closer to its original tone. Its patina is more subdued than Traditional Bronze, and it responds to the same gentle buffing.

Photo to source: new → ~15-year patina → after light buffing

On your terms

Let it age, slow it, or keep it bright.

Everyday cleaning

Wipe with a soft cloth and mild dish soap. Skip abrasive pads and harsh chemicals for routine cleaning — the patina is part of the bronze, not dirt on top of it.

Brighten it back

To restore the original luster, work the surface with an ultra-fine Scotch-Brite pad or bronze wool — bright on the high points, leaving the recesses dark so the design keeps reading. Never use steel wool, which can leave rust stains. [verify]

Slow the patina

After install, a light coat of paste wax (such as Renaissance Wax [verify product]) slows how quickly the bronze mellows — reapply occasionally.

Hold it as-is

To keep a tile close to its just-installed look, seal it with a bronze-compatible lacquer such as Incralac [verify product]. Best decided before install.

By setting

Floors and wet areas.

Floors

Foot traffic naturally polishes the high points, so floor installations stay bright where they’re walked and darken in the recesses — the pattern only reads more clearly over time.

Pools & fountains

Tiles in constant water want a periodic reseal with a bronze lacquer to keep them from darkening too quickly.
