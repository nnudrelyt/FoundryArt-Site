How we got here

Ted Lowitz built a business rooted in his own artwork. Art and commerce were both in the family — a grandmother who painted, a grandfather who kept shop — and for more than thirty-five years Ted led the studio he founded, designing tiles and teaching the people around him.

Stacza Lipinski started here in 2002 and has since worked nearly every job in the building: talking to customers and cleaning the slop sink, buffing bronze and baking birthday cakes, glazing tiles and styling photographs. When Ted retired in 2024, she bought the company and renamed it Linden Workshops, a nod to the origins of her family name.

She considers herself a steward of the three studio brands that came with it, and Ted's vision still guides how the place is run.

Everyone here makes their own work.

Three brands, three very different catalogs — but the thread running through all of them is the same four people. Each keeps a practice outside the studio, and that habit of making is what ends up in the tile.

When you choose tile, you make a decision you will live with for a long time. The decision can be daunting. You know what appeals to you today, but what will appeal to you tomorrow? At Linden Workshops, we strive to interpret timeless designs in a personal way. The patterns on our tiles appear in countless variations in art and architecture — from prehistoric stone carvings and classical friezes to modern abstract sculpture. They are timeless designs that resonate in our minds. We hope you will find our tiles to be engagingly new and yet deeply familiar.
