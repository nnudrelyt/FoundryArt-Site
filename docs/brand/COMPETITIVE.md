## Brand profiles

### Motawi Tileworks · Ann Arbor, MI · est. 1992

**Positioning:** *"Made with Heart. Meant to Last."*

- Decorative ceramic art tile sold as collectible, home accent, and gift — as much displayed as installed
- Direct e-commerce + in-person gallery and factory tours; strong community orientation
- Licensed collections (Charley Harper, Frank Lloyd Wright) give cultural cachet beyond tile
- Accessible price points; gift-buyer and DIY-friendly

> **Roadmap copy critique:** Emotional and accessible. Motawi leads with feeling over craft specifics, positioning their tiles as objects of affection as much as function. "Heart" softens the product into something personal and giftable; "Meant to Last" adds a durability promise without getting technical. A warm, approachable line built for D2C appeal.

**Gap for Foundry Art:** Entirely ceramic — no metal, no architectural material. Consumers drawn to their craft story but wanting something more material-forward have nowhere to go.

### Pratt & Larson · Portland, OR · est. 1982

**Positioning:** *"Passionate alchemists who appreciate the magic of luxury of handmade tile."*

- Premium ceramic field tile for residential and commercial projects with hundreds of glazes, field shapes, relief, mosaics, and architectural trim
- Built for the trade: dealer and showroom distribution with a limited online shop for select SKUs
- Positioned as artisan alchemists — deep on customization and technical sophistication

> **Roadmap copy critique:** Trade-facing and craft-forward, but overwrought. "Alchemists" signals technical sophistication and positions the brand as transformative rather than merely decorative — a smart choice for a designer audience. But the line tries to do too much: "passionate," "magic," "luxury," and "handmade" each pull in slightly different directions. Reads like a brand that knows who it is but hasn't edited itself down to the sharpest version of that idea yet.

**Gap for Foundry Art:** Covers surfaces — doesn't finish rooms. Designers specifying Pratt + Larson for field tile still need a metal accent source. Foundry Art is a complement, not a competitor.

### Syzygy Tile · Silver City, NM · est. 1993

**Positioning:** *"Timeless. Sophisticated. Distinctive."*

- 20-person handmade studio; bold geometric ceramic field tile and mosaics
- Sygma collection (Quasar, Nebula, Eclipse, Pulsar) — shapes hand-cut and pressed, glazes brush-applied
- Showroom-only distribution; extensive color system and mosaic configurations

> **Roadmap copy critique:** Three adjectives in search of a point of view. Confident in tone but says nothing other premium brands couldn't claim. There's no material story, no process, no place, no person. Works as a design aesthetic signal — the rhythm and spacing of the words mirrors the brand's geometric sensibility — but as a positioning statement it's almost entirely unownable.

**Gap for Foundry Art:** All ceramic, no metal vocabulary, showroom-only. Designers who want a metal punctuation point in a Syzygy installation have no option within the brand. **A Syzygy installation is an ideal pairing candidate for Foundry Art bronze accents.**

### Lunada Bay Tile · SoCal · est. 2002

**Positioning:** *"Lunada Bay Tile pushes the boundaries of tile design, helping people express their artistic visions on vertical and horizontal planes in residences, commercial spaces, and beyond."*

- Multi-material field tile and mosaics: ceramic, glass, stone, and concrete
- Specialty strength in pool, spa, and water feature tile; Tommy Bahama partnership reinforces coastal/resort positioning
- 2M+ SF in-stock; scale player with active reach toward design-forward consumers (Mistral Zellige, Palm Canyon MCM collections)
- Authorized showroom distribution only

> **Roadmap copy critique:** A mission statement wearing a tagline's clothes. "Pushes the boundaries" is among the most overused phrases in design marketing, and "vertical and horizontal planes" is technical language that flatters the brand's range while meaning very little to the buyer standing in a showroom. The line is trying to speak to architects, designers, and homeowners simultaneously and lands cleanly with none of them.

**Gap for Foundry Art:** No metal product, and trade-only distribution leaves online search entirely uncaptured — a channel gap Foundry Art is positioned to own.

## Opportunities for Foundry Art

From the Roadmap synthesis (page 32):

- **LEAN INTO MATERIALITY:** Every competitor works in ceramic, glass, or stone. Foundry Art is the only brand in the set offering solid bronze tiles. Metal is not an adjacent option for competitors — it is Foundry Art's exclusive territory.
- **FOCUS ON DISTINCTION:** All four competitors are primarily field tile brands; they cover surfaces. Foundry Art serves a fundamentally different role: it finishes, punctuates, and elevates what is already there.
- **PROMOTE ACCESSIBILITY:** Pratt + Larson, Syzygy, and Lunada Bay are showroom-only. Motawi sells direct but is ceramic. Foundry Art is the only premium bronze tile brand accessible online without a showroom visit — for consumers and designers.
- **MERCHANDISE SETS:** No competitor offers adjacent hardware. Foundry Art's bronze knobs and pulls create a rare opportunity: whole-kitchen coherence with metal tile and metal hardware from a single artisan source.
- **OWN THE HIGH END:** Foundry Art is in a class all its own. Combine 30+ years of heritage with the brand's tasteful aesthetic and customer accessibility for a brand story that's *"artisan quality meets modern convenience."*
