## On the patina + technical context

Lifted from the same doc — useful for /shop and PDP technical sections.

- **Patina:** Bronze becomes more beautiful with age. Its patina deepens and matures — time, weather, and wear each playing a part. Technically the process is oxidation: bronze responds to the oxygen in the atmosphere.
- **In a floor:** Time-worn paths appear as the tiles are gradually polished by footsteps. Like the shiny nose of a well-loved statue, this is a beautiful reward that comes only with age.
- **In wet applications or outdoors:** The natural patina accelerates and an opaque green patina ultimately develops.
- **To slow patina:** Apply a light coat of paste wax after install.
- **To stop patina:** Clear epoxy coating (similar to a kitchen faucet) or post-install lacquer.
- **To restore brightness:** Polish with a soft abrasive (Scotch Brite). For darker tiles, use copper or brass cleaner. Maintain with paste wax.

## Color of bronze (technical)

- **Traditional Bronze:** Deep colored, silicon bronze (typical of fine sculpture casting).
- **White Bronze:** Lighter, cooler tone. Inscribed with a "W" on the back.
- Both are alloys of copper and zinc — among the most durable types of bronze.
- **Important:** Not surface finishes. Both are solid materials. Even after decades of being walked on and cleaned, there is no danger of color wearing off to reveal a less appealing base metal. "Our tiles are bronze through and through."

## Texture + variation (technical)

- No two tiles are exactly alike — casting and finishing techniques purposely encourage differences.
- Rough areas are a feature, not a defect: "reminders of the many hands that make our tiles."
