# User Research

Indexed from the Roadmap deck, pages 33–69 (Website Audit, including the user-interview component).

## Methodology

- **User interviews**, conducted by The Main Sequence team
- **5 participants** matching the Foundry Art buyer profile (homeowners + design-curious professionals)
- Site shared with each; asked about impressions, feelings, and ability to discern brand/product details from the existing layout and content
- **Open-ended, conversational** format
- Goal: capture patterns and trends in feedback and perceptions of the brand and product offering

## What's working

### Product clarity
**Why it works:** all respondents were able to quickly and accurately understand the core product offering and main point of differentiation — hand-cast bronze, without prompting. Several spontaneously noted the D2C element and understood the product as one intended for consumer purchase over wholesale or B2B.

> *"It seems to be a page made to highlight that collection of specialty tiles."*
>
> *"Foundry suggests metal."*

### Strong, specific copy
The line *"Carved by hand. Cast in solid bronze. Available direct from our studio."* is specific and confident, clearly defining what the product is and how it's meaningfully different from competitors.

### Beautiful product photography
Visualizes the value Foundry Art tile brings — elevation of everything around it.

> *"These are amazing and beautiful. I'd definitely put some in my house."*
>
> *"I would want this because it's high end and unique — something you won't see in most homes."*

### Downloadable content
Having supplementary content (tear sheets, "How We Make Our Tiles", recycled content notes, install guide) offers other ways to engage beyond purchase and signals intent to buy. Customers who download can be targeted for future communications.

## What's not working

### Brand architecture
Several respondents were confused about the relationship between Lowitz & Company, Linden Workshops, and Foundry Art. This confusion gives customers pause and creates barriers to trust — they have to do mental work before engaging in the product story and purchase path. Eliminating distracting information helps customers quickly orient and feel reassured they've landed in the right place.

> *"There's a lot going on."*

### Product identity
*"Carved by Hand. Cast in solid bronze."* clearly defines the product. A line like this should be the first thing customers read. By comparison, *"Unique • Handmade • Timeless"* is true to product but overly broad and generic — could apply to almost any premium product. One respondent expected those adjectives to be visually associated with the Foundry Art wordmark, almost like a tagline.

### Elevate brand presentation
Many respondents noted a **gap in their perception of the brand based on its price point vs. the visual design of the site.** This can erode credibility and creates resistance to the price point. Customers need to *feel* the luxuriousness of the product coming through in all aspects of the site — design, copy, and product photography. They want the product to feel special so they feel special for purchasing.

> *"This (the site) doesn't feel special."*
>
> *"If you are trying to appeal to a more upscale clientele... you're appealing to them based on emotion, not price."*

### Stronger brand storytelling
Several respondents noted a desire to learn more about the brand story and the "why" behind the product. This is table stakes for a high-end, luxury product like Foundry Art Tiles — customers are purchasing on emotion over price and need a strong connection before deciding "This product is for me."

Existing copy that starts to approach this — *"When you choose tile, you make a decision you will live with for a long time."* and *"We believe how your cabinet hardware feels is as important as how it looks"* — sits too low on the page amid blocks of small, long-form copy.

> ⭐ **THE KEY QUOTE** ⭐
>
> *"When I think about something like this... I love the idea that there's a story about how this thing is made. I'm buying a piece of art... what are the artisans behind this? What are the hands that made this?"*

### Cleaner path to purchase
All respondents expressed a degree of confusion around why they had to go to a different site (Claremont Tile) to purchase. Respondents did all note they felt comfortable transacting and that both sites felt legitimate and trustworthy — but the handoff cost trust.

> *"Are the three main tiles the only three available?"*

### Photography usage
Multiple respondents were confused by the sample photos and didn't intuitively understand they were real-world examples of Foundry Art products being applied. Showing these photos in their own section, or within the context of a specific product, adds clarity, context, and dimension to the product offering rather than creating confusion.

> *"This could be its own section."*

### Elevate drawer pull offering
Copy and imagery higher up the page doesn't speak to the drawer pull offering and focuses exclusively on metal accent tiles. Most respondents did not notice the drawer pull section until prompted. This not only diminishes a core product offering but misses a huge opportunity to elevate the Foundry Art brand to a "whole room" solution focused on craft and experience.

> *"I completely missed the knob and drawer pulls at first."*

### Offer expert guidance
Several respondents noted they loved the product but felt unsure of how it was intended to be used and how much they should purchase — a barrier to purchase. Another respondent was shocked at the price point initially under the assumption that Foundry Art products were meant to cover larger surface areas, similar to other tile products. Offering downloadable guides or lookbooks bridges that gap, creates trust, and highlights the myriad ways Foundry Art products can elevate a space.

> *"I would feel comfortable buying if I knew what I needed."*
>
> *"They've answered my questions for me."*

### Create social proof
While no respondents noted this as missing, adding customer testimonials and case studies is a great way to build trust with new customers, convey the many positive attributes of the Foundry Art brand, and inspire new customers with what they can create based on the experience of others.

---

## Recommendations (Roadmap synthesis)

- **SIMPLIFY TOP-OF-PAGE:** Re-factor site header and navigation to create clarity rather than confusion.
- **CLARIFY OFFERING:** Make sure brand copy speaks equally to metal accent tiles and drawer pulls.
- **ELEVATE:** Site updates should make the brand feel high-end and luxurious, worthy of the premium price point.
- **CREATE CONTEXT:** Show real-world application of specific products on the product detail page.
- **MERCHANDISE:** Present Foundry Art as a "whole room solution" that creates an experience rather than simply a finish.
- **SHOW & TELL:** Intersperse product showcases with thoughtful elements of brand storytelling — history, process, premier customer service so customers can identify with and buy into a story, not just a product.
- **EXPERT POSITIONING:** Offering guidance on how products can be used — use this as an opportunity to forward "elevate" messaging.
- **LEAN ON PAST SUCCESS:** Highlight customer stories and experiences for those who've purchased Foundry Art products in the past.
- **CUSTOMER FOCUS:** Maintain the customer's viewpoint when drafting messaging and avoid having the site feel like a reflection of the brand's internal, company organization.

## Key insight

> *"The site is organized around the studio's internal logic rather than the customer's discovery experience. Someone arriving cold doesn't know what bronze accent tile is, why it matters, or why Foundry Art is the only place to get it. The page needs to quickly tell that story before presenting options for purchase."*

This is the single most important sentence in the entire research. Every layout, copy, and IA decision should be checked against it.

> *"I mean, now I'm thinking about accent tiles, which is something I've never really thought about before."* — proof point that, when the page lands, it really does land.
